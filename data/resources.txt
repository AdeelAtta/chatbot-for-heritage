# Resources and References for Mohenjo-daro

## Primary Sources and Research

### Archaeological Reports

The systematic documentation of Mohenjo-daro began with the formal excavations of the 1920s and continues to the present day. Key primary sources include:

**Marshall, Sir John (1931)** - "Mohenjo-daro and the Indus Civilization" - The foundational three-volume work presenting the results of initial excavations. Published by Arthur Probsthain, London. This remains the essential reference work for early archaeological discoveries at the site, though many interpretations have been revised in light of subsequent research.

**Vats, M.S. (1940)** - "Excavations at Mohenjo-daro" - A comprehensive report on excavations conducted between 1924 and 1931, providing detailed documentation of stratigraphy, architecture, and artifacts. Published by Government of India Press, Delhi. This work established many of the site interpretations that remain influential.

**Dani, Ahmad Hasan (1989)** - "Mohenjo-daro: A Great Indus City" - A modern reassessment of the site with contributions from multiple specialists. Published by Sang-e-Meel Publications, Lahore. This work provides updated perspectives on site interpretation based on research conducted after significant advances in archaeological science.

**UNESCO (1960-1970)** - "Mohenjo-daro: The Ruins and Excavations" - Reports from UNESCO missions assessing site conditions and conservation needs. Available through UNESCO documentation centers.

### Journals and Periodicals

Regular scholarly publications provide ongoing research updates:

**Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society of Pakistan** - Contains articles on Indus Valley Civilization archaeology and interpretation.

**Ancient Pakistan** - Published by the University of Peshawar, contains detailed archaeological reports and analyses.

**South Asian Studies** - International journal with articles on Harappan archaeology and related topics.

**Journal of Archaeological Science** - Scientific approaches to dating, materials analysis, and environmental reconstruction at Indus Valley sites.

**Antiquity** - International journal featuring research on Mohenjo-daro in broader comparative contexts.

## Books and Monographs

### General Overviews

**Possehl, Gregory L. (2002)** - "The Indus Civilization: A Contemporary Perspective" - Rowan Alta Mira Press. Updated synthesis of Indus Valley Civilization research, including discussion of Mohenjo-daro's place in the broader civilization.

**Kenoyer, Jonathan Mark (1998)** - "Ancient Cities of the Indus Valley Civilization" - Oxford University Press, Karachi. Comprehensive illustrated overview based on extensive archaeological research at Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, and other sites.

**Kenoyer, J.M. and Miller, H. (1999)** - "Ancient Indus Valley: New Perspectives" - SAR Press, Santa Fe. Collection of essays providing current research perspectives.

**Wright, Rita P. (2009)** - "The Ancient Indus: Tomb, Town, and the Territorial Upheavals" - Cambridge University Press. Updated analysis of Indus Valley urban development.

### Specialized Studies

**Jansen, Michael (1989)** - "Mohenjo-daro: Architecture, Water Supply, and hygienic Conditions" - in "Old Problems and New Perspectives in the Indus Valley Archaeology," Pakistan Archaeology. Technical analysis of Mohenjo-daro's engineering achievements.

**Raikes, R.L. (1967)** - "Mohenjo-daro: A New System of Watertight Dating" -补水. Original work proposing the water table theory that revolutionized understanding of Mohenjo-daro's decline.

** Mughal, Rafiq (1997)** - "The Genesis of the Indus Valley Civilization" - Sang-e-Meel Publications, Lahore. Synthesis of chronological research and regional settlement patterns.

### Conservation Literature

**UNESCO (2014)** - "The Conservation of the Ruins of Mohenjo-daro" - Technical report on conservation challenges and recommendations. Available through UNESCO documentation.

**UNESCO (2019)** - "State of Conservation Report - Mohenjo-daro" - Periodic assessment of site conditions and threats.

## Research Gaps and Opportunities

### Unanswered Questions

Significant research questions remain about Mohenjo-daro:

The undeciphered Indus script continues to limit understanding of administrative and religious systems.

Precise causes of the civilization's decline remain debated.

Social organization and governance structures require further investigation.

Understanding of trade networks and economic systems continues to develop.

### Opportunities for Contribution

Researchers and students may find opportunities in:

Environmental reconstruction using modern scientific techniques.

Comparative studies with other ancient urban civilizations.

Analysis of materials from previously excavated contexts.

Development of new conservation approaches for mud brick structures.

## Citation Guidelines

### For Academic Writing

Standard citation format for archaeological reports following the American Antiquity style guide:

**For site reports:**
Author(s). Year. Title. Publisher, Location.

Example: Marshall, J. 1931 Mohenjo-daro and the Indus Civilization. Arthur Probsthain, London.

**For journal articles:**
Author(s). Year. Title. Journal Name Volume(Issue): Page numbers.

**For book chapters:**
Author(s). Year. Chapter Title. In Book Title, edited by Editor(s), pages. Publisher, Location.

### For Educational Use

Simplified citation suitable for educational presentations:

Author/Organization. Year. Title. Available at: URL. Accessed: Date.
